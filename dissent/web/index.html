Every tool merges that into one label

When laboratories disagree about what a variant means, the public databases show you a single summary and throw the disagreement away. So does every tool built on them.

Dissent keeps it. It assembles the evidence from six public databases, works through the 28 criteria clinical geneticists use, and puts the contradictions first — including the ones between its own conclusion and the database's.

Pick a variant above to see it work.

What this does

Interpreting one variant means opening ClinVar, gnomAD, a predictor site, Open Targets and PubMed, then reconciling them by hand. The reconciliation is where the errors happen, and the tools that automate it hide that step behind a single label.

Dissent produces a draft ACMG/AMP worksheet with every criterion sourced, and puts the disagreements first: submitters who contradict each other, records with no stated method, assertions a decade old, predictors that split, frequencies that contradict a pathogenic claim, and germline criteria applied to somatic variants.

Pick one of the cases above to see it work.

Data from ClinVar, gnomAD v4, dbNSFP, Ensembl VEP, snpEff, Open Targets, dbSNP and NCBI LitVar — all via public endpoints, no API keys. Draft worksheets for research use; not a clinical determination.
